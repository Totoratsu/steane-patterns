Appendix A

Glossary

Every key term from the chapters, in alphabetical order, with the chapter that defines it.

95% interval
The range around a measured rate that would hold the underlying rate in about 95 of 100 repeats. Ch. 10
Ancilla
A helper qubit that is measured in place of the data qubits, so the check answer is learned without reading the data. Ch. 4
Backend
The machine or simulator that runs the circuits. Ch. 12
Bit flip
An X error: 0 becomes 1 and 1 becomes 0. Ch. 1
Check
A parity question about four data qubits, answered with one bit. Ch. 2
Cluster
The clashing sound of a shot where at least one logical qubit was lost. Ch. 11
CSS code
A code whose Z checks and X checks come from classical matrices; here, the same one. Ch. 2
Data error
A flip on a data qubit. It stays until something undoes it. Ch. 5
Data qubit
One of the seven physical qubits that together hold a logical qubit. Ch. 2
Decoder
A program that reads the detections and returns a guess of which errors happened. Ch. 7
Detection
A check whose answer changed since the previous round. Ch. 5
Detections view
The grid after each cell is compared with the same check in the round before. Ch. 5
Detector error model
The matrix that lists, for every possible single fault, which detections it lights. Ch. 7
Distance
The smallest number of errors that can change the logical qubit without any check noticing. Ch. 2
Grid
The space-time picture of a shot: one column per check, one row per round. Ch. 4
Hook error
An ancilla error mid-check that spreads through the CX gates to several data qubits. Ch. 6
Leg
One run of memory BP; the next leg starts from the posteriors the previous one ended with. Ch. 9
Log-likelihood ratio
The number ln((1−p)/p) that the decoder uses as the cost of blaming a fault. Ch. 7
Logical error
A shot where a decoder's predicted flip differs from the true flip in some block. Ch. 10
Logical qubit
The one qubit of information stored across the seven data qubits. Ch. 2
Major chord
The sound of a shot where both logical qubits were recovered. Ch. 11
Measurement error
A check read out wrongly. The qubits are fine; only that one answer is a lie. Ch. 5
Memory BP
Belief propagation in which each qubit blends its prior with its previous total belief. Ch. 9
Memory strength
The weight γ of that blend: 0 is plain BP, larger values trust the past more, negative values push against it. Ch. 9
Message
A number a node sends along a line: a check tells a qubit how strongly to believe it is flipped, or not flipped. Ch. 8
Mid-circuit measurement
Measuring a qubit while the circuit is still running, then using it again. Ch. 12
Min-sum
The update rule used here: a check sends the sign of the other opinions and the smallest of their strengths. Ch. 8
Noise channel
A rule that says where in the circuit a random Pauli error may be inserted, and with what probability. Ch. 6
Note
One tone for one fired check, with a pitch from its column and a waveform from its kind. Ch. 11
Optimal-decoder limit
The logical error rate of the best possible decoder for the model, computed exactly. Ch. 9
Parity check
A question of the form “is the number of 1s among these qubits even or odd?” Ch. 3
Pauli error
X, Z or Y; the three basic ways a single qubit can go wrong. Ch. 1
Pauli frame
The record of which qubits carry an X or Z error, tracked through every gate. Ch. 10
Pentatonic scale
A scale with no semitone steps, so any combination of its notes avoids the harshest clashes. Ch. 11
Phase flip
A Z error: the sign of the 1 part of the state changes. Ch. 1
Physical error probability
The number p that sets the strength of a channel; what it multiplies depends on the channel. Ch. 6
Posterior
A qubit’s total belief after the messages, as a log-likelihood ratio; negative means “flipped”. Ch. 8
Prior
The pair (data, meas) of error probabilities the decoder assumes, per Pauli type. Ch. 6
Qubit
A two-level quantum system whose state is a mix of 0 and 1. Ch. 1
Raw view
The grid of check answers exactly as measured. Ch. 5
Round
One pass over all the checks of both blocks: 12 checks. Ch. 4
Short cycle
A loop of only a few lines in the Tanner graph, along which a message returns to the node it came from. Ch. 8
Shot
One noisy run of the whole circuit, with its own randomly drawn errors. Ch. 4
Statevector
The full list of amplitudes of all qubits, whose size doubles with each qubit. Ch. 12
Superposition
A state that is a weighted sum of 0 and 1 rather than one of them. Ch. 1
Syndrome
The answers of all the checks in one round, written as a string of bits. Ch. 3
Tanner graph
A drawing of a check matrix: one node per check, one node per qubit, and a line wherever the matrix has a 1. Ch. 8
